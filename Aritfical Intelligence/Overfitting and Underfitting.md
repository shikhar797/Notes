
==Overfitting is when a model learns training data too well, including noise, leading to great training performance but poor results on new data;== ==underfitting is the opposite, where a model is too simple and fails to capture patterns in the training data, resulting in poor performance everywhere, including training==. The goal is a balanced model that generalizes well, fitting the trend without memorizing noise, which is the "just right" fit.

Overfitting (High Variance)

- **What it is:**
    
    The model is too complex and essentially memorizes the training data, including random fluctuations (noise).
    

- **Performance:**
    
    Very low error on training data but high error on new, unseen data (test data).
    

- **Analogy:**
    
    A student who memorizes answers for a specific test but doesn't understand the subject, failing on a new test.

Underfitting (High Bias)

- **What it is:** The model is too simple and fails to learn the underlying patterns or structure in the data.

- **Performance:** High error on both training and test data.

- **Analogy:** A student who barely studies and doesn't grasp basic concepts, performing poorly on any test.
